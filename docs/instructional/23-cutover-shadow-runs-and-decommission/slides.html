Cutover, Shadow Runs

Done means: the new engine billed real clients, the old one is off, and nobody noticed.

The goal

Legacy is off, and nobody noticed.

Evidence before cutover · small steps during it

Rollback defined before you need it · measure before you delete

Two kinds of evidence

Golden master (lesson 04)

New engine matches legacy on the seed scenarios.

Shadow mode

Matches legacy on real production data, at full scale, over time.

How shadow mode works

A nightly, classified diff

Account by account, every difference gets a parity-report category (lesson 04):

DOUBLE_PRECISION

ROUNDING_MODE

DAY_COUNT

ALLOCATION_PENNY

Every difference is explained (ADR or ticket), or it's a bug.

"Explained" means allowlisted

Each allowlist entry names

  • A category
  • The accounts or scenarios it covers
  • The maximum amount
  • The ADR or ticket

The report, per firm

  • Accounts matched exactly
  • Accounts that differ, by category
  • Total dollar impact

Anything unexplained fails the check and pages someone.

"Same inputs" is a trap

A position file lands between the two runs → different AUM → a difference that has nothing to do with the engine.

Snapshot the billing inputs. Run both engines against the same snapshot.

Leaving shadow mode: not on a feeling

  • A full quarter of shadow runs for all firms
  • Zero unexplained differences for two consecutive runs
  • Month-end shadow runs too, for more samples
  • Named sign-offs: product, finance or compliance, client success

After cutover: reverse shadow

Modern engine

System of record

Legacy engine

Runs in shadow for one cycle

The diff must still be zero, or every difference explained. The safety net for the first live run.

The unit of cutover is the firm

A per-firm flag: billing engine = Legacy | Modern

Microsoft.FeatureManagement, or Azure App Configuration feature flags targeting individual firms.

Evaluate the flag once, at enqueue time

Sketch · target design · StartBillingRun handler
// Decide once, when the run is enqueued, and record the decision on the run.
var engine = engineSelector.EngineFor(firmId);          // Legacy | Modern, from the per-firm flag
db.BillingRuns.Add(new BillingRun { FirmId = firmId, PeriodEnd = periodEnd, Engine = engine });

// The worker reads run.Engine. It never re-reads the flag mid-run.

Otherwise a flag flip mid-run gives you a run that's half legacy, half modern.

The flag is a controlled change

  • Flipping a firm to modern needs the same approval as a deployment
  • The flag store keeps an audit history: who changed what, and when

Reconcile billed against debited

database/billing/002-stored-procedures.sql · usp_ReconcileFeeDebits
SELECT  i.Id                        AS InvoiceId,
        a.AccountNumber,
        i.Amount                    AS InvoicedAmount,
        ISNULL(SUM(d.DebitedAmount), 0) AS DebitedAmount,
        i.Amount - ISNULL(SUM(d.DebitedAmount), 0) AS Difference
FROM dbo.Invoices i
INNER JOIN dbo.Accounts a ON a.Id = i.AccountId
LEFT  JOIN dbo.FeeDebits d ON d.InvoiceId = i.Id
WHERE i.RunId = @RunId
GROUP BY i.Id, a.AccountNumber, i.Amount
HAVING i.Amount <> ISNULL(SUM(d.DebitedAmount), 0);

A conversation for account management, with numbers.

A per-firm impact report

  • Total change in fees per quarter
  • Largest change for any single account
  • How many accounts go up, how many go down
  • Interaction with minimum fees

Then agree an effective date, and opt-in or contractual. The per-firm policy flag honours both.

The rule

Two flags. Two changes. Two sign-offs.

If invoices change on cutover day, you must know whether the engine or the policy did it.

Two items people forget

Secrets

Rotate everything ever committed: machine keys, database passwords in Web.Release.config and Web.ClientX.config. Deleting a file doesn't remove it from git history.

Retention

Archive logs, generated invoices and database backups under the regulated retention policy, with the owner's sign-off.

A migration is done when…

  • Legacy is switched off
  • The fallback route has seen zero traffic for the agreed period
  • Its data is archived under retention rules
  • Its CI job, servers, accounts and secrets are gone

Until then, you're paying for two systems.

A runbook, one per firm

Every step has:

  • An owner, named as a role, not a person
  • A time
  • A verification, with the expected result written in advance

Traps

  • Leaving shadow mode on a feeling
  • Different inputs producing false differences: snapshot the AUM
  • Re-evaluating the flag mid-run: decide at enqueue time
  • Rollback by DELETE: void, reissue, credit, reconcile
  • Fixing the bug during cutover
  • Deleting the fallback route before measuring it
  • "Decommissioned", but still paying for accounts, servers, firewall rules, CI runner
  • Assuming that deleting a file removes a secret
Interview drill

Four questions

After each question, pause and answer out loud. Then compare with the model answer.

Question 1 of 4

How do you cut over a billing system?

Answer out loud.

Model answer
  • A full quarter of shadow runs, same input snapshot, nightly classified diff
  • Exit: two consecutive runs with zero unexplained differences, named sign-offs
  • Firm by firm; flag evaluated once at enqueue time and stored on the run
  • Legacy runnable for a cycle, in reverse shadow; rollback rehearsed
Question 2 of 4

What does rollback mean once invoices have been sent?

Answer out loud.

Model answer
  • Draft: void, redo the run on legacy
  • Approved: void and reissue, with an audit link
  • Debited: compensating credit or next-period adjustment, reconciled, communicated
  • Never deletes; agreed with finance and compliance before cutover
Question 3 of 4

When is a migration done?

Answer out loud.

Model answer
  • Legacy switched off, and nobody noticed
  • Zero fallback traffic for an agreed period
  • Hidden consumers have owners and replacements
  • Data archived; CI job, servers, accounts gone; committed secrets rotated
Question 4 of 4

You found a legacy bug during the migration. When do you fix it?

Answer out loud.

Model answer
  • After cutover, behind its own per-firm flag, with business sign-off
  • Quantified: ÷ 4 → actual/365 moves a $2.5M account by $43.66 a quarter
  • Migration and behaviour changes never ship together
